**Scope:** AIMO Interpretability Challenge, **Main Track** (no size cap). Same task as the small
track: `are_robust(model_id, problems, reasoning_effort="default") -> list[bool]`, shipped as a
separate ZIP **without** `small.txt` and ranked separately.

## 0. Read this first

Ten lines of state, for whoever picks this up.

1. **The task is not to solve olympiad problems.** It is to predict, per (model, problem), whether
   that model's answer survives answer-preserving perturbations.
2. **The Main Track scores five models.** Four are the small track's checkpoints; the fifth is
   `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, and it is the only one whose `reasoning_effort` varies.
3. **The four small models need no new work** — the small track's shipped policy covers them
   (`e27k`, 0.86 there). Everything below is about the fifth model and about doing better than a
   constant on all five.
4. **Nothing has been submitted to the Main Track yet.** M11a is the first ZIP and nothing blocks it.
5. **Robustness is a property of the problem, not the model** (LF-12: 11 of 11 problems labelled
   for two or more models agree, including gpt-oss-120b against each small model). So predict once
   per problem and broadcast. A per-problem oracle scores **0.986** against the 71 official labels,
   where always-robust scores 0.732.
6. **Effort barely matters** (LF-13: 2 label flips in 22 cross-effort comparisons, both at `low`).
7. **The bottleneck is labels: there are 33 labelled problems.** Every offline lane that failed in
   both tracks failed at that n, or on the wrong population.
8. **Compute is now unlimited, and that buys exactly one thing: labels** (§4, Lane A). 500
   olympiad problems with ground-truth answers are already pooled and pinned (LF-15).
9. **Compute does not buy test-time budget.** One GPU, 3600 s, all five models, every problem
   (§3). Whatever the offline programme discovers has to fit in about one forward pass per pair.
10. **Start at §4.1 (M0) and §4.2 (M1).** The gate that can kill the programme is **G-M3**, and
    `protocols/M3_PROTOCOL.md` freezes it before any label is read.

## 3. The two budgets

This is the discipline the whole plan hangs on, and the easiest thing to get wrong now that
offline compute is free.

| | Offline (ours) | Inference (Codabench) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardware | **Unlimited** | **One** RTX PRO 6000, 96 GB |
| Time | Weeks | **3600 s for the entire run** |
| Network | Yes | None. `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1` |
| What it buys | Labels, fitted artifacts, feature selection, ablations | Nothing new |

Everything in §4 Lanes A–C spends the unlimited side. **Lane D is where a method has to survive.**
Concretely, the 3600 s covers loading five checkpoints (gpt-oss-120b is ~62 GiB in MXFP4) *and*
scoring every (model, problem) pair. The affordable shapes, cheapest first:

1. **One forward pass per pair** over the problem text (this is what `e27k` does). Always affordable.
2. **A handful of forward passes per pair** — the original plus mechanically perturbed copies, no
   generation. Affordable; this is M7 and it is the most promising untested lane.
3. **Short sampling, gpt-oss at `low` only.** Measured 1,876 generated tokens per reply at `low`
   with 98% answered and no truncation (LF-11), against 17k–26k at medium/high. k=4 on 30 problems
   is ~225k generated tokens, which is plausible but must be measured (M10) before it is shipped.
4. **Anything else — long traces, k≥8 at high effort, test-time perturbation *generation*** — does
   not fit. The small track already measured 28k tokens per problem for one such method (E14).

**Unlimited offline compute does not relax this.** It changes which cheap feature we can *choose*
with confidence, not how much we can do at score time.

### 4.1 M0 — infrastructure: real GPUs, real context, no proxy

**Why.** The L1 panel spent ~120 GPU-hours and produced one usable cell out of eight. The cause was
not GPU hours: it was a 16k context on quantised Ollama builds, which truncated 43–97% of
thinking-model replies before they reached an answer (LF-11). And gpt-oss-120b never ran at all.

**What to build.**

- vLLM, bf16 (not Q4), tensor-parallel, **32k context minimum and 65k for `high` effort**; the
  measured mean at `high` was 26,093 generated tokens with 65% still truncated.
- `gpt-oss-120b` directly, at all three efforts. The MXFP4 checkpoint the AIMO3 notebooks mount
  (`danielhanchen/gpt-oss-120b`) is already known to mount (LF-3); on adequate hardware, load it
  in bf16 or MXFP4 as the hardware allows and **record which**, because the report has to state it.
- One job runner: `jobs/panel_vllm.py`, same append-per-completion record schema as
  `jobs/ollama_panel.py` (`problem_id`, `item_id`, `sample`, `effort`, `content`, `thinking`,
  `eval_count`, `done_reason`, `error`) so `scripts/write_results.py` keeps working unchanged.
- **Resume on the key** `(problem_id, item_id, sample, effort)`, as the Ollama panel already does.
  This is what made a 34,000-second session recoverable.

**Do not** reuse the Kaggle launcher for this. `scripts/klarge.py` is a T4-fleet tool with a
weekly quota model; it stays for cheap CPU probes.

**G-M0:** one gpt-oss-120b cell over the 36 official problems, k=4, ≥ 95% of replies containing
`\boxed{}` and ≤ 5% truncated. If truncation is above that, raise the context and re-run; do not
start M2 on a truncating configuration.

### 4.2 M1 — official-family perturbations with verified answer preservation

**Why.** The organizers' variants come from symbolic chains in `problems-public`, which is private.
Their five surface families are reproducible without it, and the 453 official variant *texts* in
`aimo-interp-challenge-sample-full` are in hand as style exemplars (LF-2).

**What to generate,** per problem, 4 variants per family:

| Family | How | Answer preservation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `typos` | Mechanical: character-level noise outside math spans | **By construction.** Assert every numeric literal and every `$…$` span is unchanged |
| `rename` | Mechanical: substitute person/place/variable names from a fixed table | **By construction.** Same assertion |
| `distract` | Append one plausible but irrelevant sentence | **By construction** for the numbers, but *well-posedness* needs a check |
| `rephrase` | LLM rewrite, meaning preserved | **Needs verification** |
| `domain` | LLM rewrite into another surface domain, same math | **Needs verification** |

**Verification** is the part that decides whether M2 is worth anything:

1. Mechanical assertions first (cheap, catch most breakage).
2. For `rephrase` and `domain`: re-solve the variant with a **panel of three models from different
   families** at high effort, k=4 each. Accept the variant only if the panel's modal answer equals
   the original's known answer in all three. Reject otherwise — a rejected variant costs nothing,
   a wrong one corrupts a label.
3. **A human reads 50 accepted variants** (10 per family) and marks each answer-preserving or not.

Reuse: `datagen/perturb/transforms.py` in the small track has the mechanical machinery and the
`Variant` type, but only three non-official families — the official five are new work. Keep its
`compliance` tag: the report must be able to state exactly which families a label came from.

**G-M1:** ≥ 45 of the 50 hand-checked variants answer-preserving, and ≥ 90% of generated variants
surviving verification per family. A family below that is dropped from M2 rather than patched.

### 4.3 M2 — labels at scale, under the official rule

**Population.** The 505 pooled olympiad problems with single integer answers (LF-15:
OlympiadBench 362, AMC 83, AIME-2024 30, AIME-2026 30), plus the 36 `train-main-v2` problems as
the calibration set. **Add AIME-2025** (`MathArena/aime_2025`) — `train-main-v2` contains 2025 AIME
problems verbatim, so this is how the calibration set gets real answers instead of the
model-derived E24a key.

**Procedure,** per (problem, model, effort):

1. **Base phase:** k=8 on the original. Eligible iff base accuracy = 1.0 (the organizers' own
   curation rule — a model that cannot solve the original has no robustness to measure).
2. **Variant phase**, eligible problems only: k=4 on each of 20 accepted variants.
3. **Label:** per-family accuracy → `max_drop` = the largest drop across families → three-valued
   label with thresholds **calibrated in M3, not assumed**.

**Cells:** 4 small models at `default` + gpt-oss-120b at `low`/`medium`/`high` = 7.

**Cost,** using measured token counts (LF-11), staged so the expensive phase only runs on eligible
problems:

| Phase | Completions per cell | Tokens per completion | Tokens per cell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base, 541 problems × k=8 | 4,328 | ~10,000 | ~43 M |
| Variants, ~40% eligible × 20 × k=4 | ~17,300 | ~10,000 | ~173 M |
| **Per cell** | **~21,600** | | **~216 M** |
| **All 7 cells** | **~151,000** | | **~1.5 B** |

At a sustained 3,000 generated tokens/s per GPU (vLLM, batched, 8B bf16) that is ~140 GPU-hours;
gpt-oss-120b runs perhaps 3× slower per token, so provision **250–400 GPU-hours** for the full run
and expect 2–3 days on eight GPUs. `low` effort is roughly 5× cheaper than `high` (1,876 vs 26,093
tokens measured), so run `low` first — it is the cell with the fewest official labels (7) and the
most to gain.

**Output:** `data/labels/perturbation_rows.parquet` and `instances.parquet`, in the same schema as
the small track's `label.py` writes, so its training code reads them with no new code paths.

**The compliance line.** Labels derived from **official variant texts** (the 453 in the reference
table) are **calibration only, never training data**. Labels derived from **our own variants of
our own pooled problems** are training data, which the 26 Sep organizer message explicitly allows.
Keep the two in separate files and never concatenate them.

### 4.4 M3 — the gate: do our labels agree with the organizers'?

**Why this is the only gate that matters.** If our pipeline reproduces the official labels, we have
turned unlimited compute into 200–500 in-population labels and every lane below becomes testable.
If it does not, our labels are measuring something else and the Main Track reverts to constants.

**Method.** Run M2 on the 36 official problems for all 7 cells, then compare with the 71 official
labels: Cohen's kappa with a bootstrap CI, plus the confusion matrix and the `max_drop` scatter
(ours against the organizers' `max_drop`, which the dataset ships).

**Thresholds are calibrated here.** Fit our robust/spurious cut points so our labels best reproduce
the 71 official ones, then hold those cut points fixed for the whole 500-problem run. Report both
the fitted cut points and the official 0.10/0.25, and say how far apart they are.

**Pre-registered in `protocols/M3_PROTOCOL.md`** — population, procedure, metric and thresholds are
frozen there before any of our labels is compared with an official one. The small track lost two
weeks to selection effects that a pre-registration would have caught (its Findings 11 and 35).

**G-M3:** kappa ≥ 0.4 with a bootstrap CI lower bound > 0 on the 71 rows, **and** the `max_drop`
rank correlation positive with CI excluding 0. Pass → the 500-problem run is trustworthy; fail →
stop Lane A, ship M11a, and write the negative result up (it is a genuine contribution: "the
official labelling rule is not reproducible from surface perturbations alone").

### 4.5 M4 — what to predict: one label per problem

**Why.** LF-12 says the label is a property of the problem at n=11; the oracle is 0.986. If that
holds at n≈500, the architecture is settled: one score per problem, broadcast to every (model,
effort), and the fifth model needs no model-specific machinery at all.

**Method.** On the M2 labels: (a) re-measure cross-model agreement with a proper CI; (b) fit a
problem-level classifier on the features from Lane C, scored by **leave-one-source-out** (AIME /
AMC / OlympiadBench) and by template-cluster bootstrap, never by plain k-fold — near-duplicate
problems otherwise leak (small track, Finding 34); (c) report balanced accuracy and AUC against
the always-robust floor.

**G-M4:** cross-model agreement CI lower bound > 0.8 keeps the problem-level architecture; below
that, go back to per-(model, problem) prediction and say so.

### 4.6 M5 — is `reasoning_effort` worth conditioning on?

LF-13 measured 2 flips in 22 cross-effort comparisons, both at `low`. At n≈500 × 3 efforts, test
whether a per-effort term beats a shared one. Expected answer: no, except possibly at `low`.
Cheap, and it decides whether the shipped `are_robust` needs to read its third argument at all.

### 4.7 M6 — white-box on gpt-oss-120b, including the router

The Main Track's only unique object of study, and the strongest report material.

- **Layer probes.** Last-prompt-token hidden states, every layer, linear probe, template-grouped
  CV. Report the **layer curve**, not the best layer: picking one layer on the same data that
  scores it is exactly the artifact that inflated the organizers' own baseline (small track,
  Finding 11).
- **Mixture-of-experts routing.** gpt-oss-120b is sparse. Read `num_local_experts` and
  `num_experts_per_tok` from the checkpoint config and record them, then extract per-layer:
  router entropy, top-1 gate margin, and the expert-load distribution over the prompt. Then the
  interesting one — **expert-load divergence between a problem and its mechanical perturbation**
  (symmetrised KL). A model that answers robustly should route a renamed problem to the same
  experts. Nobody in this competition has reported that, and it is cheap at test time: two forward
  passes, no generation.
- **Attention to the perturbed span.** For `distract` variants, attention mass on the distractor
  tokens at the final prompt position. A robust model should ignore it. This is "actionable
  interpretability" in the organizers' own words.

**G-M6:** problem-level AUC ≥ 0.70 with a bootstrap CI lower bound > 0.5 under leave-one-source-out.

### 4.8 M7 — perturbation sensitivity, redone with labels

The small track's E31 measured exactly this and got AUC 0.735 — then failed its template CI and
max-T test at n=29 templates (Finding 54). That is an n problem, not a signal problem.

**Method.** For each problem: the original plus 4 mechanical perturbations (`typos`, `rename` —
both answer-preserving by construction, so generatable at test time with no LLM). Take one forward
pass each, measure drift of the final-prompt-token representation (cosine, and the drift projected
onto the answer-token unembedding direction). Fit on M2 labels.

**Why it is the best shippable candidate.** 5 forward passes per pair, no generation: it fits the
3600 s budget comfortably, it is model-internal (the track rewards that), and it has a mechanism
story — brittle answers move under surface change.

**G-M7:** same as G-M6, plus the sign must be consistent across all five models. A signal that
reverses between models is the trap that killed three small-track lanes (Findings 30, 47, 58).

### 4.9 M8 — sparse features

Train an SAE (or transcoder) on the prompt-final residual stream of one small model over the 500
problems, then test whether any sparse feature separates robust from spurious. The small track kept
this lane report-only for compute reasons; unlimited compute reopens it. Treat it as **report
material first, submission candidate second** — an SAE artifact also has to fit in the ZIP.

**G-M8:** a feature whose problem-level AUC CI excludes 0.5 after Bonferroni correction over every
feature tested. Anything weaker is a figure, not a method.

### 4.10 M9 — cross-scale transfer

Already half-measured: the L1 panel has partial Qwen3 runs at 4B / 14B / 30B-A3B / 32B (LF-11), and
their agreement with the E24a key rises with scale (0.571 → 0.929 → 0.960) while 30B-A3B, a sparse
model, sits below the dense 14B at 0.786. Re-run properly under M0, then ask the one question that
matters for cost: **does a signal fitted on 4B predict robustness for 120B?** If yes, every future
sweep runs on a 4B model.

**G-M9:** transfer AUC CI lower bound > 0.5 for at least one Lane C feature.

### 4.11 M10 — the budget audit

Measure, on one 96 GB GPU, with a warm cache and no network:

| Quantity | Why |
| --- | --- |
| gpt-oss-120b MXFP4 load time | It may be a tenth of the whole budget |
| One forward pass per problem, per model | The floor for any shipped method |
| 5 forward passes (M7's shape) | The candidate |
| k=4 sampling at `low`, 2k tokens | Is shape 3 in §3 real? |
| Total for 5 models × 30 problems | Against 3600 s, with 2× margin |

**G-M10:** the shipped policy's measured worst case ≤ 1800 s. No margin, no ship — an over-budget
run scores zero, and the small track has already paid for one packaging mistake (Finding 21).

### 4.12 M11 — the submission ladder

**M11a — built on 27 Sep, not yet uploaded.** `submission/make_m11a.py` writes
`dist/solution-m11a-constant-gptoss-main.zip` (3.2 KB: `solution.py` + `tokenizer_check.py`, no

`gpt-oss-120b` is listed outside `SPLIT_MODELS`, so it returns the constant `True` **without the
62 GiB checkpoint ever being loaded** — the best constant at every effort, and the fastest answer,
which matters because ties break on runtime. **No `small.txt`** — that file zeroed four small-track
uploads (Finding 21), and the builder asserts its absence. Contract-checked at coverage 1.0 and
0 invalid predictions through the Codabench programs (LF-16); `submission/test_m11a.py` covers
every branch on CPU.

Uploading it also measures something we do not know: the Main-Track case count, from the accuracy
denominator, exactly as the small track learned it had 14 cases.

**M11b, after G-M4.** The problem-level model from Lane C, with M11a as the fallback path inside
the same `solution.py`: any exception, any unknown `model_id`, any timeout guard → constants. The
small track's `e27d` already ships that pattern, including catching `SystemExit` from the
tokenizer guard.

**G-L3 (unchanged):** coverage 1.0, 0 invalid predictions, native `bool`s, loads in
`Dockerfile.competition`, and `unzip -l` shows no `small.txt`.

## 8. Traps

Each of these has already cost time in one track or the other.

- **A Main-Track ZIP containing `small.txt` scores 0.00.** It cost four uploads (small track,
  Finding 21). Check with `unzip -l` every time.
- **`summary.json`'s `completions_this_session` is the work list, not the work done** (LF-11).
  Count records. The coverage table in `RESULTS.md` now does this.
- **16k context truncates thinking models.** 43–97% measured (LF-11). Truncated replies have no
  answer, and "no answer" silently counts as an answer in self-consistency statistics — which is
  why `deepseek-r1:8b` shows 0.976 self-consistency and 0.173 key accuracy in the same row.
- **`AutoTokenizer` silently strips whitespace** for DeepSeek under transformers 5.13. Load through
  the small track's `datagen/tokenizer_check.py`, which refuses a tokenizer failing a round trip.
- **Never mix the two label rules** for one pair: the older `absolute_accuracy_decay` rule and
  `train-main-v2`'s three-valued `max_drop` rule (small track, Finding 44).
- **Group by problem template, not by text.** `train-main-v2` repeats templates with one constant
  swapped, under different labels (small track, Finding 34). Our own pool has near-duplicates too.
- **Kaggle accounts are shared** with another project and with a teammate's machine; RSD and Labib
  are the same accounts as MEDIUM and CHOTON elsewhere, and an account running five `kagri-*`
  kernels refuses new pushes (LF-8).
- **Never commit `Secrets.env`**, and `git grep --cached -n "KGAT_"` must print nothing before
  every commit.
